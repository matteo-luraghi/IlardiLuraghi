\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:intro}

\section{Purpose }
As the demand for skilled professionals continues to grow, connecting university students with real-world opportunities has become increasingly important. Traditionally, students search for internships through fragmented processes, often relying on personal networks or generalized job boards, which may not adequately match their skills and aspirations with the needs of companies. This inefficiency results in missed opportunities for both students and employers.\newline
Students\&Companies (S\&C) is a platform designed to address this challenge by streamlining the internship matchmaking process. It bridges the gap between students seeking internships and companies offering them. S\&C allows students to showcase their experiences and skills through CVs while enabling companies to describe their projects and requirements in depth.\newline
Additionally, S\&C supports the selection process by facilitating interviews and feedback collection. With features for proactive searching and personalized suggestions, the platform aims to create a seamless and transparent experience for all parties involved.

\section{Scope}
As the RASD describes, two main users interact with the system: Students and Companies. \newline Students are individuals seeking internship opportunities. They can create and manage their profiles by uploading their CVs. The platform will notify students of relevant internship opportunities based on their profile information and allow them to apply directly. In addition, students can track their applications' status and receive company feedback. The system also provides a Recommendation Engine to suggest internships tailored to students' qualifications and preferences. Students can actively participate by proactively searching for internships or passively by checking suggested opportunities.\newline
Companies are the organizations offering internship opportunities. They will be able to post internship vacancies, specifying the required skills, tasks, technologies, and terms. Companies can manage and edit their internship postings and review and evaluate student applications. The system will help companies identify students who best match their internship requirements through the Recommendation Engine. Companies will also be able to schedule interviews, manage the selection process, and check and respond to feedback provided by students.

\section{Overview: High-level components and their interaction}
For the S\&C platform, we will adopt a three-tier architecture, a widely used software application design that separates the system into three distinct layers:  
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Presentation Layer}: this is the user interface, where users interact with the application
    \item \textbf{Application Layer}: this layer handles data processing and application logic
    \item \textbf{Data Layer}: this is responsible for storing and managing the application's data
\end{enumerate}

The primary advantage of this architecture lies in its modularity. Each layer can be developed, maintained, and scaled independently by separate teams, ensuring greater flexibility and efficiency. Moreover, changes or scaling in one tier do not directly impact the others, making the system more adaptable to evolving requirements.

\section{Selected architectural styles and patterns}

\subsection{3-tier architecture}
The system follows a 3-tier architectural style due to its numerous benefits, with modularization into three independent layers being the most significant. These layers are:  

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Client (Presentation Tier):} handles the user interface, presented as a website
    \item \textbf{Application Server (Middle Tier):} is responsible for implementing the business logic and runs the S\&C server in a Docker container
    \item \textbf{Database (Backend Tier):} manages data storage and retrieval, operating on a database management system (DBMS)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Model View Controller pattern}
The Model-View-Controller pattern is a robust choice for implementing the S\&C platform. It is structured as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Model:} manages the application’s data, business logic, and rules
    \item \textbf{View:} handles the presentation of data to the end user
    \item \textbf{Controller:} acts as an intermediary between the Model and the View, processing user input and ensuring proper data flow and format between components
\end{itemize}

For the S\&C platform, all components except the Webapp will serve as the Model. The Webapp will handle both the View and Controller aspects, facilitating communication with the Model components and rendering the user interface by translating the data accordingly. 

This division ensures a clear separation of concerns, enhancing scalability, promoting reusability, and maintaining each module's independence, simplifying maintenance and future development.

\section{Other design decisions}
\subsection{Availability }
Load balancing is introduced to create a highly available system capable of handling many requests simultaneously. Replication is also crucial to prevent a single point of failure.

\subsection{Security }
Security is managed through a firewall, it acts as a barrier between the internal system and external threats, ensuring that only authorized traffic is allowed while safeguarding the system from malicious activities.

\chapter{Implementation, Integration and Test Plan}
\label{Implementation, Integration and Test Plan}

\section{Overview}
This section outlines the methodologies and strategies for implementing and testing the S\&C platform. The primary goal of testing is to detect and address bugs introduced during development. The planned implementation strategies are detailed, alongside an explanation of the integration process for the various code components. These measures ensure the system meets the functional and non-functional requirements defined in the initial phases of the software development lifecycle.

\section{Implementation plan}
The \textbf{top-down approach} will be used to implement and integrate the various components of the system. This approach focuses on incremental integration and testing, starting from the highest-level interfaces and progressively working down to the lower-level modules.

The process begins with developing and testing top-level components, in this case, the Webapp containing the user interface and the API to communicate with the low-level components. Drivers are used to simulate interactions with these high-level interfaces, ensuring that they function correctly.

To facilitate testing during development, placeholder stubs are introduced for lower-level components that have yet to be implemented. These stubs simulate the behaviour of the missing components, enabling continuous testing of the integrated system without waiting for all components to be ready.

As individual components are completed, they replace their corresponding stubs, progressively expanding the system's functionality. This iterative process also involves introducing new stubs as deeper system levels are developed, ensuring that integration and testing can continue seamlessly.

Once all components have been implemented and integrated, the entire system undergoes comprehensive testing to validate its full functionality and adherence to the design specifications.

The advantages of this approach are: early validation through high-level interface testing, continuous incremental testing to address issues early and reduce risks, modular flexibility for parallel development, and controlled alignment with system design and requirements throughout the process.

\section{Components Integration and Testing}
As previously stated, the chosen approach for integration and testing consists of an incremental top-down approach. The order in which modules are implemented was chosen based on the top-down philosophy (higher-level modules first), with high consideration for the convenience and priority of each module. It is more logical to develop modules in close phases when they interact with each other or are present in the same use cases.

The Webapp component is the core controller and view of the system, responsible for managing user interactions and coordinating the behaviour of all other components. Given its critical role, it will be implemented first to establish a foundation for the integration and testing of other components. While it serves as the central hub, it will not be explicitly shown in the implementation diagrams to maintain diagram clarity and focus on the functional relationships among individual components.

\section{System testing}
The S\&C platform must be thoroughly tested to ensure its functionalities meet the expectations defined during the design and development phases. Each system component will undergo rigorous testing to identify and resolve potential issues. Some components may need stub and driver implementations to simulate interactions with missing or incomplete modules during early development stages: this allows us to test the components separately and independently from one another although they interact with each other. Once individual components are validated, they are incrementally integrated into the overall system for further testing.

The primary goal of system testing is to verify that the platform fulfils both functional and non-functional requirements as specified in the RASD document. This process requires collaboration among developers, testers, and stakeholders to ensure comprehensive coverage. The following testing phases will be carried out:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Functional Testing}
This phase ensures that the platform's functionalities align with the requirements. For S\&C, this involves:
\begin{itemize}
    \item testing whether students can upload their CVs, browse and apply for internships, and participate in interviews
    \item verifying the recommendation engine’s ability to suggest internships and candidates accurately
    \item ensuring companies can create and manage internship announcements, review applications, and manage interviews effectively
    \item ensuring that both companies and students can provide feedback on each other and respond to comments
\end{itemize}
These tests will primarily follow the use cases outlined in the RASD document.

\item \textbf{Performance Testing}
The platform must handle peak loads, especially during high-traffic periods such as application deadlines. Performance testing will:
\begin{itemize}
    \item detect bottlenecks affecting response times, system throughput, and resource utilization
    \item identify optimization opportunities for algorithms managing CV matching and recommendations
    \item simulate workloads to ensure acceptable system performance under expected and peak conditions
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Usability Testing}
This phase focuses on evaluating the user interface and overall user experience. Usability tests will:
\begin{itemize}
    \item observe real users (students and companies) performing typical tasks
    \item gather feedback on the ease of use and intuitiveness of the platform's design
    \item make iterative improvements to address usability concerns
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Load Testing}
Load testing will expose the system to increasing workloads to determine its limits. For S\&C, this includes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item simulating thousands of concurrent users uploading CVs or browsing internships
    \item stressing the recommendation engine with large datasets of CVs and internship postings
    \item ensuring the system remains responsive during prolonged periods of high activity
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Stress Testing}
Stress testing will ensure the platform recovers after failures by:
\begin{itemize}
    \item overloading system resources (e.g., memory, CPU) and observing recovery mechanisms
    \item simulating unexpected network failures and verifying system stability
    \item validating the integrity of stored data after failure scenarios
\end{itemize}

\end{itemize}


\section{Additional Specifications on Testing}
To ensure continuous improvement, the development team will collect feedback at every stage of testing. This feedback will guide adjustments to the platform before deployment.

\textbf{Alpha Testing}
During alpha testing, stakeholders such as students and companies will interact with the platform in a controlled environment. Usability experts may design structured questionnaires to assess satisfaction levels and pinpoint usability issues. This phase will identify malfunctions and areas for improvement before proceeding to beta testing.

\textbf{Beta Testing}
Beta testing involves releasing the platform to a limited group of real users under production-like conditions. Key objectives include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item uncovering bugs or issues in a live environment
    \item collecting feedback on user satisfaction and system functionality
    \item logging system errors to be addressed before general release
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Post-release Monitoring}
After deployment, the platform will continue to be monitored for issues. Logs will be automatically generated and sent to developers to facilitate debugging and future updates. Regular feedback from users will further ensure that the platform evolves to meet their needs.
